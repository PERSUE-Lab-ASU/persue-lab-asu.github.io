# Privacy Education for K-12 Students

![]({{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/img/projects/privacyplay.jpg)

Children join online games and social platforms at very young ages, but they learn almost nothing about how their data can be linked together to identify them. Privacy techniques like differential privacy and k-anonymity already protect data at Apple, Google, and the US Census Bureau, yet these concepts are only taught at the university level. Most middle schoolers still believe a made-up username keeps them anonymous.

We are addressing this gap with **PrivacyPlay**, an interactive web platform that teaches differential privacy and k-anonymity to middle school students through stories and hands-on activities, such as protecting a secret vote with coin flips and watching a fake gaming account get unmasked through a linkage attack. We deployed PrivacyPlay with middle school students in an IRB-approved study and are studying how young learners reason about privacy after using it. This project is funded by the National Science Foundation (NSF).

**Methods:** interactive system development, visualization design, user studies, surveys, qualitative analysis.
